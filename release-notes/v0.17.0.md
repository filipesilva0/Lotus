Lotus now has a proper licence, clear terms and privacy rules, and safety checks around copying voices.

## Licence
- Lotus's code is now source-available under the Functional Source License (FSL-1.1-ALv2). Anyone can read and check it, nobody can sell a competing copy, and each release becomes Apache 2.0 two years later.
- The installer shows the new licence, and the old mix of MIT and Apache markers is gone.
- A written check of every engine's licence is in docs/legal/engine-licences.md.

## Model licences
- Every model on the Models page shows its licence. Models only allowed for personal use, like FLUX.2 Klein 9B, carry a **Non-commercial** badge. Models with use limits or a licence that depends on the voice say so too.

## Copying voices safely
- Before Lotus copies a voice, you confirm: "This is my voice, or I have the speaker's permission to copy it." The same applies when you replace a voice's recording or import a voice.
- Copied voices can no longer be exported to share with others.
- Every audio file Lotus makes carries a small "Made with Lotus" tag inside the file. The sound doesn't change.

## Privacy, terms and crash reports
- Settings › Privacy holds the terms of use, the privacy policy and the open-source notices. Short version: Lotus collects nothing.
- New, and off unless you turn it on: crash reports. When Lotus breaks, it shows you the exact report first. Send opens a GitHub issue in your browser that you submit yourself.
- **Copy Diagnostics** (Help menu and Settings › Privacy) copies a summary of your Lotus version, computer and engines for when you ask for help. It never includes your chats, prompts or files.
- If a window breaks, Lotus now shows a Reload button instead of a blank page.
